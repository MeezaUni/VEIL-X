## 1. Acquisition (approved subset only)

| Archive | Bytes | Official Content-Length | SHA-256 (first download) | Zip CRC-32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `subjects_0-1999_72_imgs.zip` | 2,938,553,163 | match | `29c5347f…94f9b3` | all 144,000 members OK |
| `subjects_100000-133332_5_imgs.zip` | 3,399,664,847 | match | `998253e1…84f641` | all 166,665 members OK |

- **Source:** the URLs listed in the official README of `github.com/microsoft/DigiFace1M`,
  fetched over HTTPS. The host throttles single connections to about 0.3 MB/s, so a resumable
  segmented range downloader was used. Every segment had to return `206` with a matching
  `Content-Range`.
- **No published checksum exists.** The SHA-256 values are trust-on-first-download
  fingerprints, *not* proof of authenticity. Integrity rests on three things: the exact
  official size, CRC-32 of every member, and a full decode of every image.
- **Safe extraction:** every member name had to match `<digits>/<digits>.png`. Absolute
  paths, `..`, backslashes, symlinks, special files, other file types and compression ratios
  above 200 are all refused. Extraction goes to a temporary directory and is renamed into
  place only on success. Result: **0 rejected members**.
- **Disk usage (measured, data root outside both git repositories):**
  - 5.90 GiB of archives;
  - 5.87 GiB of extracted images;
  - about 1.6 GiB of private manifests, fingerprints, pair lists and occlusion plans;
  - 0.1 GiB of private reports.

  That is **13.45 GiB** in total, with about 275 GiB of the drive left free.

**Alignment.** The mean of 1,000 random images lines up with the ArcFace 5-point template
(eyes, nose tip and mouth corners). This was a visual check, made locally and not
published. Occlusions are therefore placed with template landmarks. *Limitation:* per-image
pose variation (DigiFace renders vary pose) is not captured by a fixed template. A landmark
detector would refine placement, but none is authorised in Phase 1b.

**Content note.** The README states that each 72-image identity is rendered with 4 accessory
sets. Some source images may therefore already contain glasses or other accessories, which
partly confounds the sunglasses family (F2). This is recorded as a limitation, not filtered.

## 3. Near-duplicate validation: result and decision

The procedure follows `protocol_design.md` §8:
- **Positives:** an image vs a transformed copy of itself (JPEG, rescale, ≤ 10% crop,
  brightness/contrast, noise, flip).
- **Hard negatives:** two *different* images of the same identity.
- **Negatives:** two images of different identities.
- Thresholds were selected on **train** identities (4,000 of each kind) and confirmed on
  **val** identities (2,000 of each kind). Test data were never used.

| Rule | Train recall | Same-identity FPR (train / val) | Impostor FPR | Accepted? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best rule reaching 99% recall (Hamming ≤ 22, NCC ≥ 0.75) | 99.05% | **2.95% / 2.35%** | 0.48% | **No.** Fails the pre-set criterion (val same-identity FPR ≤ 1%) |
| Strict review rule (Hamming ≤ 14, NCC ≥ 0.90) | 89.95% (val 89.5%) | 0.10% / 0.10% | 0 / 6,000 | Review only, never removal |

**Trade-off frontier (train):**

| Same-identity FPR budget | Best recall |
|---|---|
| 0% | 84.0% |
| 0.1% | 90.0% |
| 0.5% | 95.8% |
| 1% | 96.4% |
| 2% | 98.3% |

**Exhaustive application of the strict review rule** over all 310,665 images (about 4.8 × 10¹⁰
pairs) nominated **676,350** pairs:
- 272,517 across identities in the same split;
- 401,057 across splits;
- 2,776 within an identity.

This is a **base-rate effect**: an impostor FPR invisible in 6,000 sampled pairs (about
10⁻⁵) still yields hundreds of thousands of hits among 10¹⁰ pairs of aligned synthetic
renders. The closest cross-identity candidates (Hamming 0–2) were inspected locally and were
**different synthetic people**, with mean absolute pixel differences of 47–112.

**Decision (D-029):**
- Exact pixel deduplication is applied; it found 0 duplicates.
- **No near-duplicate threshold is fixed**, and no image is removed on near-duplicate
  grounds.
- The candidate list is kept privately but is not a reviewable worklist at this volume.
- The protocol records the threshold as unset.
- Future real-photo benchmarks need a different duplicate procedure, e.g. exact hashes plus
  provenance metadata. Face-embedding similarity is excluded by instruction.

## 6. Occlusion plans

For each of the 5 held-out folds, condition plans exist for train, val, cal (seen families
only), test-ID (seen) and test-OOD (held-out family on every occluded side). They cover both
test_balanced and test_far, plus a combined-occlusion stress plan on test_balanced.

Every occluded side records:
- family, level, severity, visibility ratio and seed;
- the sampled parameters, the physical flag and a recipe hash.

The recipe hash binds the real source-pixel hash. Isolation is asserted per plan
(`assert_plan_isolation`) and re-checked by the integration test. Aggregated visibility
statistics per family and level are in the summary JSON (section `occlusion`).

**Scale (real run):**
- **6,129,500** occluded samples recorded across 5 folds × 7 role/pair-set combinations.
- 143,080 stress pairs.
- Plans take 0.52 GiB (gzip JSONL) in the private data root.

**Checks:**
- Strata are exactly balanced (e.g. 105,285 per stratum in each train plan).
- In each fold's train, val and cal plans, the held-out family never appears.
- In test-OOD plans every occluded pair contains it. Example: 143,080 F1 sides over 143,080
  F1-fold test pairs.

**Mean visibility ratio by family and level** (all folds and roles pooled):

| Family | low | medium | high |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 mask | 0.615 | 0.493 | 0.418 |
| F2 sunglasses | 0.910 | 0.861 | 0.784 |
| F3 hand/object | 0.898 | 0.783 | 0.644 |
| F4 random patch | 0.794 | 0.588 | 0.362 |
| F5 extreme lighting *(photometric, non-physical)* | 0.626 | 0.509 | 0.384 |

Visibility falls monotonically with level for every family. Sunglasses cover a small share
of the face region by construction, so F2 is the mildest physical family. Severity is therefore
**not** comparable across families by level name alone; analyses should use the recorded
continuous severity.
