## B. Viva questions with evidence-based answers

1. **Why not train or fine-tune the recognition model?**
   The research question is about *decision-level* risk control on top of strong existing
   backbones. Freezing them isolates calibration effects and avoids confounding.
   *Evidence:* Phase 0–2 design decisions (D-037, D-040).

2. **What exactly does the final decision use? Isn't it a dual-backbone system?**
   No. A3 uses M1: the AdaFace cosine and the AdaFace feature norm, with quality tertile strata.
   ArcFace was only compared in fusion log-loss, and earlier documents overstated this.
   *Evidence:* corrected in D-052 and traced in code (`evaluate_phase3.py`, `features.LADDER`).

3. **Your learned visibility worked on masks and sunglasses. Why call the result negative?**
   The registered C3 criterion required no fold to be significantly worse and fewer violations.
   On held-out random patch, A4 reached 8.52 % sFNMR against 2.12 % for A3, and it was worse in
   3/5 folds. A method that fails on one unseen occluder class cannot be trusted on occluders we
   did not test.

4. **Isn't the failure just a bad estimator?**
   Phase 4 fixed the estimator's bias (94.6 % → 21.9 % overestimation), and the ground-truth
   oracle was also tested. Neither beat A3 on the never-seen occluder. The limitation is in
   visibility-stratified decisions, not only in the estimator.

5. **How do you know E1 (hair) is truly unseen?**
   It was defined at registration, before any Phase 4 result. It was never rendered in
   development and never used in training, selection or calibration (`docs/phase4_protocol.md`
   §2).

6. **Doesn't LTT guarantee 5 %? Why does it fail?**
   LTT guarantees hold under exchangeability with the calibration distribution. Unseen occluders
   and a different identity population violate that assumption. The violations are evidence
   *about* the assumption, not a bug in LTT.

7. **What is the population-shift finding, and is it real?**
   - On 72-image identities, A3's sFNMR was 5.36–6.34 % on seen families, with every CI
     excluding 5 % (Phase 4).
   - A pre-registered replication on 4,000 untouched identities reproduced it: 4/5 cells
     violated (5.15–6.49 %). Clean FNMR was 4.60 % vs 1.12 % on the calibration-matched population.
   - The proposed cause, a calibration set that is 98 % 5-image identities, is a post-hoc
     explanation.

8. **Why didn't you just recalibrate on 72-image identities?**
   That would be a new calibration system. It needs its own development protocol and an
   untouched evaluation; the Phase 6 stop conditions excluded it. It is the first future-work
   item.

9. **How did you avoid p-hacking?**
   - pre-registered protocols, committed before data access;
   - one logged access per test set (`results/test_runs.jsonl`);
   - follow-up re-uses labelled;
   - Holm correction;
   - negative results reported;
   - the replication registered and pushed before the download.

10. **Your aggregate false-match rates are fine. Why worry about the low-quality tail?**
    In the ~1 % of pairs below the calibration support, A3's sFMR was 18–42 % on development
    data, and non-face pairs were decided MATCH with p = 1. Stratum-level control does not imply
    control within a tail. The app abstains there, and the frozen research system is reported
    unchanged.

11. **Is the app ready for deployment?**
    No. It is a local research demonstration with honest abstention and explicit limitations.

12. **What about real, physical occlusions?**
    Not evaluated. IJB-C is the best candidate (occlusion annotations, a 1:1 protocol). Access
    requires an eligibility-gated request; the checklist is in
    `docs/physical_occlusion_readiness.md` §7.

13. **Privacy and ethics?**
    - Synthetic faces for all confirmatory work. LFW is used under academic authorisation.
    - No images, embeddings or identity lists are in git.
    - The app is loopback-only, with no telemetry, and deletes uploads.
    - 1:1 only: no identification or surveillance capability.

14. **Why is the visibility map still in the app if it doesn't help?**
    As an *explanation* signal. It is useful to a human (where the occluder probably is). It is
    computed after the decision and tested not to influence it.

15. **What would you do differently?**
    - Define the deployment population first, and calibrate there.
    - Constrain fused scores to be monotone and support-aware.
    - Include input-support checks from the start.
    - Seek physical-occlusion data access early.
